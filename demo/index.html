About Carnegie Mellon

Founded in 1900 by industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie, Carnegie Mellon University began as the Carnegie Technical Schools — a place where working-class Pittsburghers could gain practical skills for the steel age. Today it stands as one of the world's premier research universities.

CMU's unique interdisciplinary culture fuses technology, science, business, public policy, the humanities, and the fine arts. The result is an environment where creative problem-solvers — engineers who compose, artists who code, scientists who lead — thrive together.

With campuses and research facilities in Pittsburgh, Silicon Valley, Qatar, Rwanda, and more than a dozen other locations around the globe, CMU's reach is truly worldwide.

School of Computer Science

Offers rigorous programs in civil, electrical, chemical, and biomedical engineering. Known for close industry ties and hands-on project culture.

Ranked among the global elite for MBA and Ph.D. programs. Tepper students leverage data science and analytics in every business discipline.

Student Experience

Campus Life

Life at CMU extends far beyond the classroom. From hackathons to improv shows, robotics demos to cultural festivals — the Pittsburgh campus buzzes with energy year-round.

500+ Student Orgs

From academic clubs and cultural groups to the award-winning The Tartan newspaper and the famous "Booth" competition during Spring Carnival — there's a community for everyone.

Research Opportunities

Undergraduates regularly publish research papers, contribute to national labs, and work alongside Nobel laureates through CMU's SURA and SURF programs.

Pittsburgh — A Living Lab

Pittsburgh itself is a classroom. Once a steel city, it's now a model for tech-driven urban revival. CMU's proximity to Schenley Park and the Strip District adds vibrancy to student life.

Athletics & Recreation

The Tartans compete in NCAA Division III across 17 varsity sports. The Cohon University Center offers Olympic pools, climbing walls, and fitness studios for all students.

Arts & Culture

Performances by the School of Drama and music ensembles, gallery shows by the College of Fine Arts, and public art installations dot the campus all year.

Global Community

Students from 120+ countries bring a unique global perspective. International cultural festivals, language exchanges, and global study-abroad programs enrich campus every semester.
